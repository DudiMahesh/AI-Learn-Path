## UNIT 1: Diversity in Living World

### 1.1 Classification Systems
- Two Kingdom Classification (Linnaeus): Plantae and Animalia.
- Five Kingdom Classification (Whittaker, 1969): Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.
- Three Domain System (Woese): Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.
- Binomial nomenclature (Linnaeus): Genus + Species; written in italics or underlined.

### 1.2 Kingdom Monera
- Prokaryotes: no membrane-bound nucleus, 70S ribosomes, peptidoglycan cell wall.
- Bacteria shapes: Coccus (spherical), Bacillus (rod), Vibrio (comma), Spirillum (spiral).
- Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae): photosynthetic, fix nitrogen (Nostoc, Anabaena).
- Mycoplasma: smallest living cells, no cell wall.

### 1.3 Kingdom Protista
- Unicellular eukaryotes.
- Chrysophytes (diatoms): silica cell wall (frustules), diatomaceous earth.
- Dinoflagellates: red tide (Gonyaulax), bioluminescence.
- Euglenoids: Euglena – both autotrophic & heterotrophic (mixotrophic).
- Slime moulds: Myxomycota; form plasmodium; reproduce by spores.
- Protozoans: Amoeba (pseudopodia), Paramoecium (cilia), Plasmodium (sporozoans).

### 1.4 Kingdom Fungi
- Heterotrophs (saprotrophic, parasitic, or symbiotic).
- Cell wall: chitin.
- Stored food: glycogen.
- Types: Phycomycetes (Rhizopus, Mucor), Ascomycetes (Penicillium, Aspergillus, yeast),
  Basidiomycetes (Agaricus/mushroom, Puccinia), Deuteromycetes (Alternaria, Colletotrichum).
- Lichen: symbiosis of fungus + alga (fungus provides anchorage/water; alga provides food).
- Mycorrhiza: symbiosis of fungus + plant root (improves mineral absorption).

### 1.5 Kingdom Plantae
- Algae: Chlorella, Spirogyra, Ulva (green); Fucus (brown); Polysiphonia (red).
- Bryophytes: amphibians of plant kingdom; no vascular tissue; Liverworts, Mosses.
- Pteridophytes: first vascular plants; no seeds; Ferns (Dryopteris), Selaginella.
- Gymnosperms: naked seeds; Cycas, Pinus, Gnetum.
- Angiosperms: enclosed seeds; most evolved; monocots & dicots.

### 1.6 Kingdom Animalia
- Porifera: Sycon, Spongilla; canal system; choanocytes; no true tissues.
- Coelenterata: Hydra, Aurelia; cnidoblasts; radial symmetry.
- Platyhelminthes: flatworms; Fasciola (liver fluke), Taenia (tapeworm); acoelomate.
- Aschelminthes: Ascaris; pseudocoelomate; complete digestive system.
- Annelida: Earthworm (Pheretima); metamerism; closed circulatory system.
- Arthropoda: largest phylum; Cockroach (Periplaneta); open circulatory system; exoskeleton.
- Mollusca: Pila (apple snail), Octopus; mantle; open circulatory system.
- Echinodermata: Starfish (Asterias); water vascular system; deuterostome.
- Chordata: notochord, dorsal nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits.

## UNIT 2: Structural Organisation in Animals and Plants

### 2.1 Tissues
Plant tissues:
- Meristematic: actively dividing (apical, lateral, intercalary).
- Permanent: Simple (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma) and Complex (xylem, phloem).

Animal tissues:
- Epithelial: Squamous, cuboidal, columnar, ciliated, glandular.
- Connective: Loose, dense, adipose, blood, cartilage, bone.
- Muscular: Striated (voluntary), smooth (involuntary), cardiac.
- Neural: Neurons and neuroglia.

### 2.2 Earthworm (Pheretima posthuma)
- Hermaphrodite; 14–16 segments bear reproductive organs.
- Male pores on segment 18; spermathecal openings on 5–9.
- Clitellum at segments 14–16 (secretes cocoon).
- Closed circulatory system; no true respiratory organ (exchange through moist skin).
- Typhlosole: fold in intestine for increased absorption.

### 2.3 Cockroach (Periplaneta americana)
- Open circulatory system: blood (haemolymph) in haemocoel.
- Malpighian tubules: excretion (uricotelic).
- Compound eyes; tympanal organ (hearing).
- 3 pairs of legs (6 legs); 2 pairs of wings.

## UNIT 3: Cell: Structure and Function

### 3.1 Cell Theory
- Schleiden (1838): all plants made of cells.
- Schwann (1839): all animals made of cells.
- Virchow (1855): Omnis cellula e cellula (cells from cells).
- Exception: viruses.

### 3.2 Prokaryote vs Eukaryote

| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Nuclear membrane | Absent | Present |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S (cytoplasm); 70S (organelles) |
| Cell division | Binary fission | Mitosis/meiosis |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Circular, no histones | Linear, with histones |

### 3.3 Cell Organelles (NEET Key Points)

Mitochondria:
- Site of aerobic respiration (Krebs cycle + oxidative phosphorylation).
- Double membrane; inner membrane = cristae; matrix contains enzymes.
- Has own 70S ribosomes and circular DNA → endosymbiont theory.
- "Powerhouse of the cell."

Chloroplast:
- Site of photosynthesis.
- Thylakoids (stacked = grana): light reactions.
- Stroma: dark reactions (Calvin cycle).
- Has own 70S ribosomes and DNA.

Ribosome:
- Site of protein synthesis.
- Prokaryote: 70S = 50S + 30S.
- Eukaryote cytoplasm: 80S = 60S + 40S.

Golgi Body (Golgi Apparatus):
- Modification, packaging, secretion of proteins/lipids.
- Forms lysosomes.
- cis face (receives) → trans face (ships out).

Lysosome:
- Contain hydrolytic enzymes (acid hydrolases, pH ~5).
- "Suicidal bags" (autolysis).
- Role: intracellular digestion, autophagy.

Centrosome:
- Present in animal cells and lower plants.
- Contains two centrioles at right angles.
- Forms spindle fibres during cell division.
- ABSENT in higher plant cells.

### 3.4 Cell Division

Mitosis (M-phase):
- PMAT: Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.
- Produces 2 identical diploid cells.
- Significance: growth, repair, asexual reproduction.

Meiosis:
- Two divisions: Meiosis I (reductive) + Meiosis II (equational).
- Produces 4 haploid cells.
- Crossing over in Prophase I (pachytene) → genetic variation.
- Significance: maintains chromosome number, genetic diversity.

## UNIT 4: Plant Physiology

### 4.1 Transport in Plants

Osmosis: movement of water through semi-permeable membrane from low solute (high ψw) to high solute (low ψw) concentration.
- Water potential (ψw) = ψs + ψp.
- Turgor pressure: pressure potential (ψp); builds up in plant cells.
- Plasmolysis: loss of water → cell membrane shrinks away from cell wall.
- Imbibition: absorption of water by solid matrices (seeds, wood).

Mineral Transport:
- Apoplast pathway: through cell walls (no crossing membranes).
- Symplast pathway: through cytoplasm via plasmodesmata.
- Casparian strip in endodermis forces minerals into symplast.

Translocation (phloem):
- Mass flow hypothesis (Munch).
- Source (leaf) to sink (root/fruit).
- Phloem sap: sucrose (most), amino acids.

### 4.2 Mineral Nutrition

Essential macronutrients: N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S (C, H, O from air/water).
Essential micronutrients: Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, Mo, B, Cl, Ni.

Nitrogen fixation:
- Biological: Rhizobium (legume symbiosis), Azotobacter/Azospirillum (free-living), Anabaena (BGA).
- Industrial: Haber process (N2 + H2 → NH3).
- Enzyme: Nitrogenase (requires O2-free environment; leghaemoglobin provides this in root nodules).

### 4.3 Photosynthesis

Light Reactions (thylakoid membrane):
- PS II → PS I → NADPH + ATP.
- Photolysis of water: 2H2O → 4H+ + 4e- + O2 (at PS II).
- Z-scheme of electron transport.

Dark Reactions / Calvin Cycle (stroma):
- CO2 fixation: CO2 + RuBP → 2 PGA (RuBisCO enzyme).
- Reduction: PGA → G3P (uses ATP + NADPH).
- Regeneration: G3P → RuBP.
- Net: 3CO2 → 1 triose phosphate.

C4 Plants:
- Kranz anatomy (bundle sheath + mesophyll cells).
- Initial CO2 fixation by PEP carboxylase → OAA → Malate (mesophyll).
- CO2 released to bundle sheath for Calvin cycle.
- Examples: Maize, Sugarcane, Sorghum.
- No photorespiration; more efficient in hot, dry conditions.

CAM Plants: Crassulacean Acid Metabolism; stomata open at night.
Examples: Opuntia (cactus), Pineapple.

### 4.4 Respiration

Glycolysis (cytoplasm):
- Glucose → 2 Pyruvate; Net gain: 2 ATP + 2 NADH.

Krebs Cycle (matrix):
- Pyruvate → Acetyl CoA (decarboxylation).
- 2 CO2 + 3 NADH + 1 FADH2 + 1 GTP per acetyl CoA.
- 2 turns per glucose → 6 CO2 total.

Electron Transport Chain (inner mitochondrial membrane):
- NADH → 3 ATP; FADH2 → 2 ATP.
- Total ATP from complete oxidation of glucose = 36–38 ATP.
- Terminal electron acceptor: O2 → H2O.

Fermentation (anaerobic):
- Yeast: Pyruvate → Ethanol + CO2.
- Muscle: Pyruvate → Lactic acid.

### 4.5 Plant Growth Regulators

| Hormone | Site of Production | Key Functions |
|---------|-------------------|---------------|
| Auxin (IAA) | Shoot apex | Cell elongation, apical dominance, root initiation |
| Gibberellin (GA) | Young leaves, roots | Stem elongation, seed germination, bolting |
| Cytokinin | Root apex | Cell division, delay senescence, fruit development |
| ABA | Mature leaves | Stomatal closure, seed dormancy, stress response |
| Ethylene | All tissues (ripening fruit) | Fruit ripening, abscission, promotes senescence |

## UNIT 5: Human Physiology

### 5.1 Digestion and Absorption

Digestive Enzymes:

| Location | Enzyme | Substrate | Product |
|----------|--------|-----------|---------|
| Saliva | Salivary amylase (ptyalin) | Starch | Maltose |
| Stomach | Pepsin (from pepsinogen) | Proteins | Peptones + Proteoses |
| Stomach | HCl (pH 2) | Activates pepsinogen | — |
| Pancreas | Trypsin, Chymotrypsin | Proteins | Peptides |
| Pancreas | Pancreatic amylase | Starch | Maltose |
| Pancreas | Pancreatic lipase | Emulsified fats | FA + Glycerol |
| Intestine | Maltase | Maltose | 2 Glucose |
| Intestine | Lactase | Lactose | Glucose + Galactose |
| Intestine | Peptidases | Dipeptides | Amino acids |

Bile: Produced by liver, stored in gallbladder. Contains bile salts (emulsify fats) and pigments (bilirubin, biliverdin). NOT an enzyme.

Absorption:
- Glucose, amino acids: active transport (Na+ dependent).
- Fats: form chylomicrons → lacteals (lymph) → blood.
- Water: osmosis in large intestine.

### 5.2 Circulatory System

Heart:
- 4 chambers: Right Atrium → Right Ventricle → Pulmonary artery (deoxygenated blood) → Lungs.
- Lungs → Pulmonary vein (oxygenated) → Left Atrium → Left Ventricle → Aorta → Body.
- SA node: pacemaker (60–80 bpm); generates cardiac impulse.
- AV node → Bundle of His → Purkinje fibres.

Cardiac Cycle (0.8 s):
- Atrial systole: 0.1 s.
- Ventricular systole: 0.3 s.
- Joint diastole: 0.4 s.
- Cardiac output = SV × HR = 70 mL × 72 = ~5 L/min.

Heart sounds:
- Lubb (S1): closure of AV valves (start of ventricular systole).
- Dubb (S2): closure of semilunar valves (end of ventricular systole).

ECG waves:
- P wave: atrial depolarization.
- QRS complex: ventricular depolarization.
- T wave: ventricular repolarization.

Blood Cells:
- RBC: biconcave, no nucleus (mature), haemoglobin, 120 days lifespan.
- WBC (leucocytes): Neutrophils (most common, phagocytes), Eosinophils (allergy), Basophils (histamine), Monocytes → Macrophages, Lymphocytes (B cells: antibodies; T cells: cellular immunity).
- Platelets (thrombocytes): cell fragments; role in clotting; 1–2 lakh/mm³.

Blood Groups (ABO):
- IA and IB codominant; both dominant over i.
- Group A: genotype IAIA or IAi; antigen A; antibody anti-B.
- Group B: IBIB or IBi; antigen B; antibody anti-A.
- Group AB: IAIB; both antigens; no antibodies (universal recipient).
- Group O: ii; no antigens; both antibodies (universal donor).

Rh Factor:
- Rh+ has D antigen; Rh- does not.
- Erythroblastosis foetalis: Rh- mother, Rh+ foetus → 2nd pregnancy affected.

### 5.3 Breathing and Gas Exchange

Mechanism:
- Inspiration: diaphragm contracts (flattens), intercostal muscles contract → thoracic volume increases → pressure decreases → air rushes in.
- Expiration: diaphragm relaxes, thoracic volume decreases → pressure increases → air expelled.

Lung volumes (NEET):
- Tidal Volume (TV): 500 mL (normal breathing).
- Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV): ~2500 mL.
- Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV): ~1000 mL.
- Residual Volume (RV): ~1100 mL.
- Vital Capacity (VC) = TV + IRV + ERV = 4000 mL.
- Total Lung Capacity = VC + RV = 5100 mL.

Gas Transport:
- O2 transport: 97% by haemoglobin (oxyhaemoglobin); 3% dissolved.
- CO2 transport: 70% as bicarbonate (HCO3-); 23% as carbamino-haemoglobin; 7% dissolved.
- Bohr effect: CO2 ↑ → pH ↓ → Hb affinity for O2 ↓ → O2 released.

### 5.4 Excretion

Nephron (functional unit of kidney):
- Glomerulus: filtration (blood → Bowman's capsule); process = ultrafiltration.
- PCT: reabsorption of 80% NaCl, water, glucose, amino acids.
- Loop of Henle: concentration of urine; creates osmotic gradient.
- DCT: selective reabsorption; aldosterone acts here (Na+ reabsorption, K+ secretion).
- Collecting duct: ADH increases water reabsorption → concentrated urine.

Hormonal Regulation:
- ADH (antidiuretic hormone / vasopressin): increases water reabsorption.
- Aldosterone (from adrenal cortex): increases Na+ reabsorption.
- Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF): decreases Na+ reabsorption (opposes aldosterone).
- Renin-Angiotensin mechanism: low blood pressure → renin → angiotensin II → aldosterone.

Excretory products:
- Ammonotelic (NH3): aquatic organisms (fish, amphibian larvae).
- Ureotelic (urea): humans, mammals, frogs.
- Uricotelic (uric acid): birds, reptiles, insects.

### 5.5 Nervous System

Neuron structure:
- Dendrites → Cell body → Axon → Synaptic knob.
- Myelinated: rapid conduction (saltatory); white matter.
- Non-myelinated: slow conduction; grey matter.

Resting membrane potential: -70 mV (inside negative).
Action potential: Na+ rushes in → depolarization (+40 mV) → K+ rushes out → repolarization.

Synapse: Axon terminal → synaptic cleft → postsynaptic membrane.
- Neurotransmitters: Acetylcholine (cholinergic), Noradrenaline (adrenergic).

Reflex Arc: Receptor → Afferent → Spinal cord → Efferent → Effector.
Types: Conditioned (learned) vs Unconditioned (inborn) reflexes.

Brain regions:
- Cerebrum: voluntary actions, intelligence, memory.
- Cerebellum: coordination, balance, posture.
- Medulla oblongata: involuntary actions (breathing, heart rate, swallowing).
- Hypothalamus: thermoregulation, hunger, thirst, links nervous + endocrine.

### 5.6 Endocrine System

Key Hormones (NEET Table):

| Gland | Hormone | Function |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Hypothalamus | RH/IH | Controls pituitary |
| Pituitary (anterior) | GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, Prolactin | Growth, stimulates other glands |
| Pituitary (posterior) | ADH, Oxytocin | Water reabsorption; uterine contraction |
| Thyroid | T3, T4 (thyroxine) | Metabolic rate, growth |
| Thyroid | Calcitonin | Lowers blood Ca2+ |
| Parathyroid | PTH | Raises blood Ca2+ |
| Adrenal cortex | Glucocorticoids (cortisol), Mineralocorticoids (aldosterone) | Stress, Na+ balance |
| Adrenal medulla | Adrenaline, Noradrenaline | Fight-or-flight |
| Pancreas | Insulin (β cells), Glucagon (α cells) | Lower/raise blood glucose |
| Gonads | Testosterone, Estrogen, Progesterone | Reproductive functions |
| Pineal | Melatonin | Circadian rhythm |

## UNIT 6: Reproduction

### 6.1 Reproduction in Organisms
- Asexual: single parent; genetically identical offspring (clones).
  Methods: Binary fission (Amoeba), Budding (Yeast, Hydra), Fragmentation (Spirogyra), Sporulation (Rhizopus).
- Sexual: two parents; genetic variation; gamete formation and fusion (syngamy).

### 6.2 Flowering Plant Reproduction
- Stamen: filament + anther (produces pollen = male gametophyte).
- Carpel: stigma + style + ovary (contains ovule = female gametophyte/embryo sac).
- Pollen grain: 2-celled (vegetative + generative); generative divides to form 2 male gametes.
- Embryo sac (mature): 8 nuclei, 7 cells.
  Central cell: 2 polar nuclei → endosperm (3n after triple fusion).
  Egg cell: 1 cell → embryo (2n after fertilization).

Double fertilization (unique to angiosperms):
- One male gamete + egg cell → zygote (2n).
- Other male gamete + 2 polar nuclei → primary endosperm nucleus (3n).

### 6.3 Human Reproductive System

Male:
- Testes (in scrotum, 2-3°C below body temp): sperm + testosterone production.
- Seminiferous tubules: spermatogenesis (spermatogonia → primary spermatocyte → secondary spermatocyte → spermatid → sperm).
- Sertoli cells: nourish sperm; Leydig cells: testosterone.

Female:
- Ovaries: oogenesis + estrogen/progesterone.
- Oogenesis: oogonium → primary oocyte (arrested in Prophase I) → secondary oocyte (arrested in Metaphase II until fertilization).
- Menstrual cycle (28 days): Menstrual (1–5), Follicular/Proliferative (6–13), Ovulation (14), Luteal/Secretory (15–28).
- LH surge triggers ovulation.
- Corpus luteum (after ovulation): secretes progesterone.

## UNIT 7: Genetics

### 7.1 Mendelian Genetics

Laws:
1. Law of Dominance: One allele masks the other in F1.
2. Law of Segregation: Alleles separate during gamete formation.
3. Law of Independent Assortment: Genes on different chromosomes assort independently (F2 = 9:3:3:1).

Ratios:
- Monohybrid F2: 3:1 (phenotype); 1:2:1 (genotype).
- Dihybrid F2: 9:3:3:1.
- Testcross: 1:1.

Deviations from Mendelian ratios:
- Incomplete dominance: 1:2:1 (Snapdragon).
- Codominance: 1:2:1 (ABO blood groups, IA and IB codominant).
- Epistasis (recessive): 9:3:4.
- Complementary genes: 9:7.
- Duplicate dominant: 15:1.

### 7.2 Molecular Basis of Inheritance

DNA structure:
- Double helix (Watson & Crick, 1953).
- Antiparallel strands: 5'→3' and 3'→5'.
- A-T (2 H-bonds), G-C (3 H-bonds).
- Chargaff's rule: A=T, G=C in any DNA.

Replication: Semi-conservative (Meselson & Stahl, 1958).
- DNA polymerase III: main enzyme; 5'→3' direction only.
- Leading strand: continuous; Lagging strand: Okazaki fragments.
- Primase: lays RNA primer; DNA ligase: joins Okazaki fragments.

Transcription:
- Template strand → mRNA.
- RNA polymerase; no primer needed; 5'→3'.
- Start codon: AUG (methionine); Stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA.

Translation:
- mRNA → Protein; occurs on ribosomes.
- tRNA (anticodon) brings amino acids.
- 64 codons; 61 sense + 3 stop; genetic code is degenerate & universal.

Mutations:
- Point mutation: change in single nucleotide (e.g., sickle cell anemia: GAG→GUG; Glu→Val).
- Frameshift: insertion/deletion shifts reading frame.

### 7.3 Sex Determination & Linkage

Sex determination:
- XX-XY: humans (males XY, females XX).
- ZZ-ZW: birds (females ZW, males ZZ).
- Turner syndrome: 45, XO (female, sterile).
- Klinefelter syndrome: 47, XXY (male, sterile).
- Down syndrome: Trisomy 21; 47 chromosomes.

Sex-linked traits (X-linked):
- Haemophilia, Colorblindness: X-linked recessive.
- Carrier females: XHXh (phenotypically normal).
- Affected male: XhY; Affected female: XhXh.

## UNIT 8: Ecology

### 8.1 Ecological Levels
Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere.

### 8.2 Biotic Interactions

| Interaction | Species A | Species B | Example |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Mutualism | + | + | Mycorrhiza, Lichen, Cleaner fish |
| Commensalism | + | 0 | Orchid on tree, Cattle egret |
| Parasitism | + | - | Plasmodium in humans |
| Predation | + | - | Lion-Deer |
| Competition | - | - | Two species for same resource |
| Amensalism | 0 | - | Penicillium vs bacteria |

### 8.3 Ecosystem

Energy flow (10% Law, Lindemann 1942):
- Producers → Primary consumers → Secondary → Tertiary.
- Only 10% energy transferred to next level.
- Energy pyramid is ALWAYS upright.

Productivity:
- GPP (Gross Primary Productivity): total photosynthesis.
- NPP = GPP - Respiration: available to consumers.
- Secondary productivity: production by consumers.

Ecological pyramids:
- Pyramid of Numbers: can be inverted (parasites on trees).
- Pyramid of Biomass: inverted in aquatic ecosystems.
- Pyramid of Energy: ALWAYS upright (2nd law of thermodynamics).

### 8.4 Biodiversity & Conservation

Biodiversity types:
- Genetic diversity: variations within a species.
- Species diversity: variety of species in an area.
- Ecosystem diversity: variety of habitats and ecosystems.

Hotspots: Areas with high endemism and biodiversity under threat.
- India: Western Ghats + Sri Lanka, Eastern Himalayas, Indo-Burma, Sundaland.
- World: 34 hotspots identified.

Conservation:
- In-situ: within natural habitat (National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Biosphere Reserves, Sacred Groves).
- Ex-situ: outside habitat (Zoological parks, Botanical gardens, Seed banks, cryopreservation).

Extinction causes: HIPPO — Habitat loss (most important), Invasive species, Pollution, Population (human overexploitation), Overexploitation, climate change.

## UNIT 9: Biotechnology

### 9.1 Tools of Biotechnology

Restriction endonucleases:
- Cut DNA at specific palindromic sequences.
- EcoRI: cuts at G↓AATTC → sticky ends (4-base overhang).
- SmaI: cuts at CCC↓GGG → blunt ends.
- Sticky ends are preferred in cloning (easier ligation).

Vectors:
- Plasmid: circular DNA, antibiotic resistance genes, ori, MCS.
- Bacteriophage: lambda phage (λ phage).
- Cosmids: hybrid of plasmid + phage.
- BAC (Bacterial Artificial Chromosome), YAC (Yeast Artificial Chromosome): for large inserts.
- Ti plasmid: from Agrobacterium tumefaciens; used to transfer genes to plants.

PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction):
- Denaturation: 94°C (strands separate).
- Annealing: 50–60°C (primers bind).
- Extension: 72°C (Taq polymerase synthesizes DNA).
- n cycles → 2ⁿ copies of target DNA.
- Taq polymerase: thermostable, from Thermus aquaticus.

Gel Electrophoresis:
- DNA is negatively charged → migrates from cathode (−) to anode (+).
- Smaller fragments migrate faster (farther from wells).
- Stained with ethidium bromide; viewed under UV light.

### 9.2 Applications

Genetically Modified Organisms:
- Bt Cotton (Bollguard): Cry proteins (Bacillus thuringiensis) → kills bollworm (Pectinophora).
- Bt Brinjal: resistance to shoot and fruit borer.
- Golden Rice: β-carotene (Vitamin A precursor) via phytoene synthase gene from daffodil and lycopene cyclase.
- Flavr Savr Tomato: antisense RNA for polygalacturonase → delayed ripening.

Insulin Production:
- Human insulin (humulin) produced in E. coli.
- A and B chain separately → combine with disulfide bonds.
- Previously pig/cow insulin used → immune reactions.

Gene Therapy:
- ADA (Adenosine Deaminase) deficiency: first gene therapy (1990).
- Lymphocytes isolated → ADA gene introduced → returned to patient.

Molecular Diagnostics:
- PCR: detection of HIV, TB, genetic diseases.
- ELISA: detect antibodies or antigens (e.g., HIV test).
- DNA fingerprinting: VNTR (Variable Number Tandem Repeats); Alec Jeffreys (1985).

Human Genome Project (HGP):
- Started 1990; completed 2003.
- ~3.1 billion base pairs; ~30,000 genes (later revised to ~20,000–25,000).
- 99.9% identical between humans.
- Tools: Shotgun sequencing, ESTs (Expressed Sequence Tags).
