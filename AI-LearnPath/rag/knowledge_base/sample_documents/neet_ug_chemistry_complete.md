## Unit 1: Some Basic Concepts in Chemistry

### 1.1 Matter and its nature
Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. Chemistry studies the composition, structure, properties and changes of matter.

Classification of matter:
- Pure Substances: Elements (Fe, O2, P4, S8), Compounds (H2O, NaCl).
- Mixtures: Homogeneous (air, brine), Heterogeneous (sand + water).
- Element: one kind of atom. Atomicity = number of atoms per molecule (He = 1, O2 = 2, O3 = 3, P4 = 4, S8 = 8).
- Compound: two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio.
- Mixture: physically mixed, variable composition.

States of matter:
- Solid: Fixed shape and volume, negligible compressibility.
- Liquid: Takes container shape, fixed volume, very low compressibility.
- Gas: Takes container shape and volume, high compressibility.

### 1.2 Measurement in chemistry & Significant figures
- SI Base Units: metre (m), kilogram (kg), second (s), kelvin (K), mole (mol), ampere (A), candela (cd).
- Conversions: 1 L = 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³ = 1000 mL; K = °C + 273.15; 1 atm = 101325 Pa = 760 mm Hg = 760 torr; 1 bar = 10⁵ Pa.
- Significant Figures rules: All non-zero digits are significant; zeros between non-zero digits are significant; leading zeros are not significant; trailing zeros after decimal are significant.

### 1.3 Laws of chemical combination
- Conservation of mass (Lavoisier): Mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
- Definite proportions (Proust): A pure compound always contains the same elements in the same mass ratio (H:O in water = 1:8).
- Multiple proportions (Dalton): If two elements form more than one compound, masses of one element combining with a fixed mass of the other are in a small whole-number ratio (CO vs CO2).
- Gay-Lussac's law of gaseous volumes: Gases react in simple whole-number volume ratios at the same T and P.
- Avogadro's law: Equal volumes of all gases at the same T and P contain equal numbers of molecules (22.4 L at STP = 1 mol).

### 1.4 The mole concept & Stoichiometry
- 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number Na).
- Moles n = mass / molar mass = N / Na = V(STP) / 22.4 L.
- Percentage composition = (mass of element in 1 mol / molar mass) × 100.
- Empirical formula = simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. Molecular formula = (Empirical formula) × n.
- Limiting reagent: The reactant consumed first, which limits the product formed. Divide moles by stoichiometric coefficient; smallest ratio marks the limiting reagent.
- Concentration terms:
  - Mass % (w/w) = (mass solute / mass solution) × 100 [Temp independent]
  - Mole fraction χ1 = n1 / (n1 + n2) [Temp independent]
  - Molarity M = moles solute / L solution [Temp dependent]
  - Molality m = moles solute / kg solvent [Temp independent]
  - Dilution: M1V1 = M2V2.

## Unit 2: Atomic Structure

### 2.1 Subatomic particles & Early atomic models
- Electron: Thomson cathode ray tube, e/m = 1.76 × 10¹¹ C/kg. Millikan oil-drop gave charge (-1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C), mass = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg.
- Proton: Goldstein canal rays, mass = 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.
- Neutron: Chadwick, mass = 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.
- Rutherford's alpha scattering: Small, dense, positive nucleus; atom mostly empty space.

### 2.2 Electromagnetic radiation & Photoelectric effect
- c = νλ, wavenumber ν̄ = 1/λ.
- Planck's quantum theory: E = hν = hc/λ (h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s).
- Photoelectric effect: hν = hν₀ + ½mv² = W₀ + KE_max. Threshold frequency ν₀.

### 2.3 Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom
- Quantized angular momentum: mvr = nh / 2π (n = 1, 2, 3...).
- Radius: r_n = 0.529 × (n² / Z) Å.
- Velocity: v_n = 2.18 × 10⁶ × (Z / n) m/s.
- Energy: E_n = -13.6 × (Z² / n²) eV = -2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ × (Z² / n²) J.
- Rydberg equation: 1/λ = R_H Z² (1/n₁² - 1/n₂²), R_H = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹.
  - Lyman series: n₁ = 1 (Ultraviolet)
  - Balmer series: n₁ = 2 (Visible)
  - Paschen series: n₁ = 3 (Infrared)
  - Brackett series: n₁ = 4 (Infrared)
  - Pfund series: n₁ = 5 (Far Infrared)
- Spectral lines for transition from level n to ground state: n(n - 1) / 2.

### 2.4 Quantum mechanical model & Quantum numbers
- de Broglie: λ = h / mv = h / p.
- Heisenberg uncertainty: Δx · Δp ≥ h / 4π.
- Quantum numbers:
  - Principal (n): Shell, size, energy (n = 1, 2, 3...). Max electrons = 2n².
  - Azimuthal (l): Subshell, shape (l = 0 to n - 1; s, p, d, f). Angular momentum = √[l(l + 1)] h/2π.
  - Magnetic (m_l): Orientation (-l to +l). Orbitals in subshell = 2l + 1.
  - Spin (m_s): Spin (+½, -½). Spin-only magnetic moment μ = √[n(n + 2)] BM.
- Electronic filling: Aufbau principle ((n + l) rule), Pauli exclusion principle (max 2 electrons with opposite spins), Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity.
- Stability of half-filled and fully-filled orbitals: Cr (3d⁵ 4s¹), Cu (3d¹⁰ 4s¹).

## Unit 3: Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure

### 3.1 Ionic bonding & Lattice enthalpy
- Octet rule: Atoms lose, gain, or share electrons to attain 8 valence electrons.
- Lattice enthalpy: Born-Haber cycle for NaCl: ΔfH° = S + IE + ½D + ΔegH + (-U).
- Fajan's rules (covalent character in ionic bonds): Small cation, large anion, high ionic charge, pseudo-noble gas configuration (18 e⁻).

### 3.2 Covalent bonding, VSEPR & Hybridisation
- Electronegativity: F (4.0) > O (3.5) > N (3.0) ≈ Cl (3.0) > Br (2.8) > C (2.5) > H (2.1).
- VSEPR theory: Repulsion order: lp-lp > lp-bp > bp-bp.
  - 2 pairs: Linear (BeCl2, CO2, 180°)
  - 3 pairs: Trigonal planar (BF3, 120°), Bent (SO2, <120°)
  - 4 pairs: Tetrahedral (CH4, 109.5°), Pyramidal (NH3, 107°), Bent (H2O, 104.5°)
  - 5 pairs: Trigonal bipyramidal (PCl5, 90°, 120°), See-saw (SF4), T-shaped (ClF3), Linear (XeF2)
  - 6 pairs: Octahedral (SF6, 90°), Square pyramidal (BrF5), Square planar (XeF4)
- Hybridisation: sp (linear), sp² (trigonal planar), sp³ (tetrahedral), sp³d (TBP), sp³d² (octahedral). H = ½ [V + M - C + A].

### 3.3 Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT) & Hydrogen bonding
- Bond order = ½ (Nb - Na). Stable if Bond order > 0.
- MO configuration: O2 (16 e⁻) has 2 unpaired electrons in π*2px, π*2py → Paramagnetic.
- Hydrogen bonding: F-H···F > O-H···O > N-H···N. Intermolecular raises boiling point; intramolecular (o-nitrophenol) lowers boiling point.

## Unit 4: Chemical Thermodynamics

### 4.1 First Law & Enthalpy
- ΔU = q + w (w = -P_ext ΔV).
- Enthalpy H = U + PV; ΔH = ΔU + Δng RT.
- Cp - Cv = R (for 1 mol ideal gas).
- Hess's law: ΔrH° = ΣΔfH°(products) - ΣΔfH°(reactants). Standard ΔfH° of pure elements = 0.

### 4.2 Second Law, Entropy & Gibbs energy
- Entropy ΔS = q_rev / T. For spontaneous process: ΔStotal = ΔSsys + ΔSsurr > 0.
- Gibbs energy: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS.
  - ΔG < 0: Spontaneous.
  - ΔG = 0: Equilibrium.
  - ΔG > 0: Non-spontaneous.
- ΔG° = -RT ln K = -2.303 RT log K = -nFE°cell.

## Unit 5: Solutions
- Henry's law: p = KH χ. Higher KH = lower gas solubility.
- Raoult's law: pA = χA pA°, pB = χB pB°.
- Ideal vs Non-ideal solutions:
  - Positive deviation (ΔHmix > 0, ΔVmix > 0): Ethanol + Acetone; forms minimum boiling azeotrope.
  - Negative deviation (ΔHmix < 0, ΔVmix < 0): Chloroform + Acetone; forms maximum boiling azeotrope.
- Colligative properties:
  - Relative lowering of vapour pressure: (p° - p)/p° = i χ₂.
  - Elevation of boiling point: ΔTb = i Kb m.
  - Depression of freezing point: ΔTf = i Kf m.
  - Osmotic pressure: Π = i CRT.
- van 't Hoff factor i: Dissociation i = 1 + (n - 1)α; Association i = 1 - α(1 - 1/n).

---

## Unit 6: Equilibrium
- Law of chemical equilibrium: Kp = Kc (RT)^Δng.
- Le Chatelier's principle: Shifts to counteract changes in concentration, pressure, and temperature.
- Ionic equilibrium: Ostwald's dilution law α = √(K/C). pH = -log[H+].
- Buffer solutions (Henderson-Hasselbalch):
  - Acidic: pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]).
  - Basic: pOH = pKb + log([salt]/[base]).
- Solubility product Ksp: AB → s²; AB2 → 4s³; A2B3 → 108s⁵. Precipitation when Qsp > Ksp.

---

## Unit 7: Redox Reactions and Electrochemistry
- Oxidation = loss of electrons / increase in ON. Reduction = gain of electrons.
- Standard electrode potential E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode.
- Nernst equation: E = E° - (0.0591 / n) log Q at 298 K.
- Kohlrausch's law: Λm° = ν+ λ+° + ν- λ-°.
- Faraday's laws: w = Z I t = (E / 96500) I t.
- Batteries: Lead storage battery (Pb + PbO2 + 2H2SO4 → 2PbSO4 + 2H2O), H2-O2 fuel cell.

---

## Unit 8: Chemical Kinetics
- Rate law: Rate = k [A]^p [B]^q. Overall order = p + q.
- Zero order: [A] = [A]₀ - kt; t½ = [A]₀ / 2k.
- First order: k = (2.303 / t) log([A]₀ / [A]); t½ = 0.693 / k.
- Arrhenius equation: k = A e^(-Ea / RT); log(k₂ / k₁) = (Ea / 2.303 R) [(T₂ - T₁) / (T₁T₂)].
- Catalyst lowers activation energy Ea without changing ΔH or K.

---

## Unit 9: Classification of Elements and Periodicity
- Modern periodic law: Properties are periodic functions of atomic numbers.
- Trends: Atomic radius decreases across period, increases down group.
- Ionisation enthalpy: Increases across period, decreases down group. Exceptions: Be > B, N > O.
- Electron gain enthalpy: Cl > F (compact 2p in F causes repulsion), S > O.

---

## Unit 10: p-Block Elements (Groups 13–18)
- Group 13: Inert pair effect (Tl+ stable). Boric acid H3BO3 is monobasic Lewis acid. Diborane B2H6 has two 3c-2e- banana bonds.
- Group 14: Catenation C >> Si > Ge. Diamond (sp³, insulator), Graphite (sp², conductor), Fullerene C60.
- Group 15: NH3 basicity, Ostwald process for HNO3, passivates Al and Cr.
- Group 16: H2SO4 Contact process with V2O5 catalyst.
- Group 17: Halogen oxidising power F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2. Bond dissociation Cl2 > Br2 > F2 > I2.
- Group 18: XeF2 (linear), XeF4 (square planar), XeF6 (distorted octahedral), XeO3 (pyramidal).

---

## Unit 11: d- and f-Block Elements
- Transition elements: Partially filled d-orbitals. Variable oxidation states (Mn up to +7). Colored ions via d-d transitions. Spin magnetic moment μ = √[n(n + 2)] BM.
- Potassium dichromate K2Cr2O7: 2CrO4²⁻ (yellow) + 2H+ ⇌ Cr2O7²⁻ (orange) + H2O.
- Potassium permanganate KMnO4: Acidic (n = 5), Neutral (n = 3), Strongly alkaline (n = 1).
- Lanthanoids & Actinoids: Lanthanoid contraction causes Zr ≈ Hf. Actinoids show wider oxidation states.

---

## Unit 12: Coordination Compounds
- Werner theory: Primary (ionisable) and secondary (coordination number) valencies.
- Isomerism: Ionisation, Solvate, Linkage, Coordination; Geometrical (cis/trans), Optical (enantiomers).
- Crystal Field Theory (CFT): Octahedral field splits d into t2g (-0.4Δo) and eg (+0.6Δo).
- Spectrochemical series: I- < Br- < Cl- < F- < OH- < H2O < NH3 < en < CN- < CO.
- Synergic bonding in metal carbonyls strengthens M-C and weakens C-O.

---

## Unit 13: Purification and Characterisation of Organic Compounds
- Methods: Sublimation, steam distillation (o-nitrophenol), fractional distillation, chromatography.
- Lassaigne's test: N gives Prussian blue Fe4[Fe(CN)6]3; S gives violet with nitroprusside; Halogens give AgX precipitate with AgNO3.
- Quantitative estimation: Dumas (%N from N2 volume at STP), Kjeldahl (%N from ammonia titration), Carius (%Cl, %Br, %S).

---

## Unit 14: Some Basic Principles of Organic Chemistry (GOC)
- IUPAC nomenclature priority: -COOH > -SO3H > -COOR > -COCl > -CONH2 > -CN > -CHO > >C=O > -OH > -NH2 > C=C > C≡C.
- Electronic effects: Inductive (-I: -NO2 > -CN > -COOH > -X; +I: alkyl groups), Resonance, Hyperconjugation (α-H count).
- Intermediates: Carbocation stability 3° > 2° > 1°; Carbanion stability 1° > 2° > 3°.

---

## Unit 15: Hydrocarbons
- Alkanes: Wurtz reaction, decarboxylation with soda lime, free radical halogenation. Ethane staggered vs eclipsed conformations.
- Alkenes: Markovnikov addition of HX; Kharasch peroxide effect (anti-Markovnikov, with HBr only). Ozonolysis cleaves C=C.
- Alkynes: Terminal alkynes are acidic (form silver acetylide with Tollens'). Hydration (Kucherov) gives aldehydes/ketones. Cyclic polymerization of ethyne gives benzene.
- Aromatic: Hückel's (4n + 2)π rule. Electrophilic aromatic substitution: nitration, halogenation, Friedel-Crafts alkylation/acylation.

---

## Unit 16: Organic Compounds Containing Halogens
- Haloalkanes: SN1 (carbocation, racemisation, 3° favoured) vs SN2 (single step, Walden inversion, 1° favoured).
- Elimination (Saytzeff rule): Dehydrohalogenation with alcoholic KOH gives more substituted alkene.
- Haloarenes: Low reactivity toward nucleophilic substitution due to resonance and sp² C; activated by o/p-nitro groups.

---

## Unit 17: Organic Compounds Containing Oxygen
- Alcohols: Lucas test (3° immediate turbidity, 2° in 5 min, 1° on heating). Dehydration gives alkenes.
- Phenol: More acidic than alcohols (resonance stabilised phenoxide). Kolbe's reaction gives salicylic acid; Reimer-Tiemann gives salicylaldehyde.
- Ethers: Williamson synthesis (R-X 1° + R'ONa). Cleavage with HI: Anisole gives phenol + CH3I.
- Aldehydes & Ketones: Nucleophilic addition. Aldol condensation (needs α-H); Cannizzaro reaction (no α-H, disproportionation); Iodoform test (positive for CH3-C=O and CH3-CH(OH)-).
- Carboxylic acids: Acidity increased by -I groups (CCl3COOH > CH3COOH). HVZ reaction (α-halogenation with X2/red P).

---

## Unit 18: Organic Compounds Containing Nitrogen
- Amines: Basicity in water: (C2H5)2NH > (C2H5)3N > C2H5NH2 > NH3 (2° > 3° > 1°). Aniline is much weaker base due to resonance.
- Tests: Carbylamine test (1° amines give foul smelling isocyanide); Hinsberg test distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3° amines.
- Diazonium salts: Sandmeyer reaction (CuCl/HCl gives chlorobenzene), Gattermann reaction, azo coupling with phenol gives orange dye, with aniline gives yellow dye.

---

## Unit 19: Biomolecules
- Carbohydrates: D-(+)-Glucose (aldohexose, 4 chiral carbons, α and β anomers). Sucrose is non-reducing invert sugar; Maltose and Lactose are reducing disaccharides. Starch (amylose + amylopectin) and Cellulose.
- Proteins: α-amino acids, peptide bonds. Primary, secondary (α-helix, β-sheet), tertiary, and quaternary structures. Denaturation.
- Nucleic acids: DNA double helix (A=T, G≡C), RNA (uracil replaces thymine). Phosphodiester linkages.
- Vitamins: Fat soluble (A, D, E, K), Water soluble (B, C).

---

## Unit 20: Principles Related to Practical Chemistry
- Detection of functional groups: Alcoholic -OH (ceric ammonium nitrate), Phenolic -OH (neutral FeCl3 violet), Aldehyde (Tollens' silver mirror, Fehling's red ppt), Carboxylic acid (NaHCO3 effervescence).
- Salt analysis: Cation group reagents: I (dil HCl), II (H2S acidic), III (NH4Cl + NH4OH), IV (H2S alkaline), V ((NH4)2CO3), VI (Na2HPO4), 0 (NaOH).
- Titrations: KMnO4 self-indicator with oxalic acid and Mohr's salt in dil H2SO4.
